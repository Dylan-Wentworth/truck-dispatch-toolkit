# Pre-Book Carrier Vetting Checklist

Run this before tendering any load to a carrier you have not worked with
in the last 90 days. Every check is free through FMCSA. Skipping steps is
how double-brokering and cargo theft happen.

## 1. Authority and identity (Company Snapshot / Licensing and Insurance)

- [ ] Legal name and DBA on the rate confirmation match FMCSA exactly
- [ ] USDOT and MC numbers match the carrier's paperwork and W-9
- [ ] Operating authority status is **AUTHORIZED** for property (and for
      household goods if applicable), not revoked or pending revocation
- [ ] Authority type is carrier, not broker-only or freight forwarder-only
- [ ] Phone number and email on file with FMCSA match what the carrier
      gave you. A mismatch is the single most common double-broker flag
- [ ] Physical address is a real terminal or residence, not a virtual
      office, UPS Store box, or a recently changed address with no history

## 2. Insurance (FMCSA Licensing and Insurance portal)

- [ ] BIPD coverage meets your requirement (750k minimum by law,
      1M standard for most shippers, 5M for hazmat oil)
- [ ] Cargo insurance is in force and meets the load value
- [ ] BMC-84 / BMC-85 or BMC-91 filings are active, no pending cancellation
- [ ] Insurance filing date is not brand new alongside brand new authority

## 3. Safety record (Company Snapshot / SAFER)

- [ ] Safety rating is Satisfactory or None. Never load a carrier rated
      **Conditional** without written management approval, and never an
      Unsatisfactory carrier
- [ ] Out-of-service rates are near or below the national average for
      vehicle and driver inspections
- [ ] Crash history reviewed for fatalities and a pattern of tow-away crashes
- [ ] No active out-of-service order (check the OCO/OSO listing)

## 4. Authority age and change history

- [ ] Authority older than 90 days. Under 90 days requires extra checks:
      call the FMCSA-listed phone number, verify the truck VIN, and get a
      photo of the truck door logo with the load paperwork
- [ ] No recent authority transfer, name change, or reinstatement after a
      revocation. This is the classic chameleon carrier pattern: an old,
      shut-down carrier's identity revived under new ownership

## 5. Double-broker red flags (any two, stop and escalate)

- [ ] Contact email is a free webmail account that does not match the
      company name, when the carrier claims to be an established fleet
- [ ] Carrier pressures you to book immediately or offers to take the load
      well under market rate with no negotiation
- [ ] Carrier cannot name the driver, truck number, and trailer number at
      booking time
- [ ] Carrier refuses live tracking (MacroPoint, Trucker Tools, or ELD share)
- [ ] The person who booked the load is not the person who shows up in
      call records, or the callback number differs from FMCSA records
- [ ] Fuel advance requested before pickup is confirmed with BOL in hand

## 6. Before you send the rate confirmation

- [ ] Callback verification completed on the FMCSA-listed number
- [ ] Driver name, phone, truck and trailer numbers collected
- [ ] Certificate of insurance requested directly from the insurance agent
      (never accept a COI sent only by the carrier as sole proof)
- [ ] Carrier packet complete: signed agreement, W-9, authority letter,
      COI, and references on file

Sources: FMCSA Company Snapshot (safer.fmcsa.dot.gov), Licensing and
Insurance portal, and the FMCSA carrier registration pages. Verify live;
records change daily.
