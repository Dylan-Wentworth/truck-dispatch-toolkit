# First load with a new broker checklist

Running a first load for a broker you have never worked with is a
credit decision with a truck attached. Work this list before the
rate con is signed. Most of it takes ten minutes, and it is the ten
minutes that decide whether the next thirty days are paperwork or
collections.

## Verify the broker

- [ ] **MC number checks out.** Look the broker up in FMCSA
      Licensing and Insurance: the MC on the rate con matches the
      legal name, the authority type is broker, and the authority
      is active, not revoked or pending revocation.
- [ ] **Bond is current.** Broker BMC-85 bond shows as filed and in
      force in the same FMCSA record. No bond, no load.
- [ ] **Address is real.** The address on the rate con matches the
      FMCSA record and maps to an actual office, not only a mailbox
      store. A mismatch between the rate con and the federal record
      is a stop-and-ask, every time.
- [ ] **Age of authority.** Authority granted in the last 90 days is
      not an automatic no, but it means first-load precautions below
      are mandatory, not optional.

## Check the credit

- [ ] **Factoring company approves them.** Submit the broker to
      your factoring company before booking and get the approval in
      writing. A decline or a low credit limit is the single most
      useful data point you will get about a new broker.
- [ ] **Days-to-pay picture.** Ask around and check the broker's
      reputation where carriers share it. Slow payers rarely hide
      it for long.
- [ ] **Your own scorecard starts now.** Open a history for this
      broker (the `broker` command scores it later): record the
      agreed terms on day one, so the first invoice can be graded
      against what was promised, not what was remembered.

## Put the money terms in writing

- [ ] Payment terms on the rate con: net days, quick-pay percent if
      any, and the exact invoicing instructions (email, portal, or
      mail) and what documents they require.
- [ ] Accessorial terms on the rate con: detention free time and
      hourly rate, layover per day, TONU amount, lumper reimbursed
      at cost. See the negotiation playbook's clause list.
- [ ] The legal name and MC on the rate con match the FMCSA record
      exactly. Invoices addressed to the wrong entity age quietly.

## Run the quick-pay math before you need it

If the load is big and the broker is new, decide in advance how you
will carry the invoice. The `broker` command's quick-pay math shows
what a 2 or 3 percent quick-pay fee annualizes to; compare that
against your factoring rate and against simply waiting out the
terms. A 2 percent fee to be paid 20 days early is an effective
annual rate over 37 percent, which is sometimes still the right
call on a first load with an unproven broker, but it should be a
decision, not a reflex.

## First-load precautions

- [ ] **First load, smaller load.** Where the freight allows it,
      make the first load a modest one. Trust is built at amounts
      you can afford to chase.
- [ ] **Confirm pickup details with the shipper's paperwork,** not
      only the broker's email: address, hours, reference numbers.
- [ ] **Paperwork home the same day.** Signed BOL in hand and POD
      sent the day of delivery starts the payment clock clean; a
      POD that drifts for a week becomes a 37-day invoice on 30-day
      terms.
- [ ] **Calendar the due date.** The day the invoice is sent, set
      the follow-up for the day it is due. Chasing on day 31 is a
      routine email; chasing on day 60 is a collections problem.
